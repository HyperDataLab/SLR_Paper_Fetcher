# SAFFRON 4.0 Term Extraction and Taxonomy Extraction

## What the official Term Extraction module does, and what its scores mean

### Takeaway
SAFFRON is a corpus-level English term and taxonomy tool. Its extracted terms and scores are useful as candidate metadata and filter facets, but the scores express salience/relevance within the submitted corpus under the configured scoring method; they are not calibrated probabilities that a paper is scientifically relevant or that a term is objectively correct.

### Cited Findings
- The official Wiki says SAFFRON modules were developed for English; its term extraction target is domain-characteristic single- or multiword expressions found frequently and relevant to the input corpus. [Wiki Home](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki) · [Term Extraction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.1.-Term-Extraction)
- Candidate identification extracts noun phrases subject to configured n-gram bounds, POS constraints, and minimum corpus frequency; subsequent stages score candidates, rank/aggregate scores, and retain top N. [Term Extraction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.1.-Term-Extraction)
- FORMATS lists defaults `maxTerms=100`, `ngramMin=1`, `ngramMax=4`, `minTermFreq=2`; `method` is `voting` or `single`; voting defaults to `comboBasic`, `weirdness`, `totalTfIdf`, `cValue`, `residualIdf`. It supports thresholds, blacklist terms/files, maxDocs, and `oneTopicPerDoc` (false by default). [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md)
- Available scoring functions include `comboBasic`, `weirdness`, `totalTfIdf`, `cValue`, `residualIdf`, `avgTermFreq`, `basic`, `novelTopicModel`, `postRankDC`, and `relevance`. The Wiki warns frequency-based measures favor single-word phrases and advises combining scoring principles or setting minimum term length to two for less single-word bias. [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md) · [Term Extraction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.1.-Term-Extraction)
- `terms.json` provides `term_string`, total corpus `occurrences`, document `matches`, and `score` (FORMATS says score is between 0 and 1 and represents importance to this corpus); `doc-terms.json` maps term to document and per-document occurrence count. Morphological variants are also described, but the field spelling differs across docs (`mv_list` in FORMATS versus `morphologicalVariationList` on Wiki). [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md) · [Saffron outputs](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/3.-Saffron-outputs)
- The README lists CLI corpus inputs as TXT/DOC/PDF directories or archives, JSON metadata corpus, or URL crawl; it documents running the full CLI pipeline and Docker execution module-by-module or as a pipeline. The input-format document also permits each JSON document to supply contents, file, URL, or ID (at least one), plus metadata/date/authors. [README](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron) · [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md)

### Inferences
- For SLR API filters, persist terms as *paper-level candidate labels* with provenance and counts, not as unquestioned controlled vocabulary. Preserve term string, extraction version/config, corpus/run ID, score/method, per-paper occurrence count, and review state so consumers can distinguish machine suggestions from accepted taxonomy labels.
- Score values from different corpora/configurations should not be compared as a global confidence without empirical calibration. The official definition is corpus-relative, and term inclusion changes with corpus composition, frequency minimum, ranking method, and top-N cut-off.
- Start with abstracts plus title as a low-noise, reproducible pilot; full text can expand coverage but may introduce references, headers, OCR/layout artifacts, and boilerplate unless the PDF-to-text pipeline is quality-gated. This is an integration recommendation, not an SAFFRON guarantee.

### Gaps
- The public docs do not establish a universally validated threshold for “good term,” guarantee domain-specific recall/precision, or explain that the score is calibrated across different runs.
- Documentation supports English models (including OpenNLP English POS/lemmatizer defaults); it does not establish robust Vietnamese extraction. Treat non-English use as unsupported absent local evaluation.
- Public docs describe PDF as an accepted corpus input but do not specify OCR behavior, extraction-quality reporting, or how malformed/scanned PDFs are handled.

## What Taxonomy Extraction represents and the meaning of its edge score

### Takeaway
Taxonomy output is a single-root, unique-parent hierarchy over the supplied candidate concepts. SAFFRON estimates pairwise child-to-parent likelihoods with a supervised classifier and searches for a globally acceptable tree; `linkScore` is described as the likelihood that a node’s link to its parent/root is correct, not a human validation or universal confidence guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- The official Wiki defines a taxonomy as a partial order with transitivity, unique-parent, and single-root constraints. “General” and “specific” are domain-dependent; classical taxonomy uses hypernymy/hyponymy, while the page also notes part-of or other domain-relevant relations may be considered. [Taxonomy Construction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.3.-Taxonomy-Construction)
- Construction has two stages: pairwise scoring estimates p(child ⊑ parent), then search maximizes a taxonomy likelihood. Pairwise scoring is supervised: an SVM is trained on concept-pair features and taxonomy examples (e.g. TexEval); class +1 means the first concept is a child of the second, and the model’s +1 probability is used as the estimate. [Taxonomy Construction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.3.-Taxonomy-Construction)
- Features include label/string features, corpus-frequency features, word embeddings, and lexical/WordNet features. The Wiki describes use of GloVe embeddings and WordNet hypernym features; FORMATS exposes optional GloVe/hypernym files, feature selection, and search algorithms `greedy`, `beam`, `mst`, with `greedy` documented as default. [Taxonomy Construction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.3.-Taxonomy-Construction) · [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md)
- `taxonomy.json` nests nodes through `root` and `children`; each node has `score` (weight assigned to its term) and `linkScore` (likelihood the link from the term to its root is correct). Deprecated `status` and `parent` fields are documented. JSON is default; `taxonomy.returnRDF=true` selects RDF. [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md) · [Saffron outputs](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/3.-Saffron-outputs)
- The standalone taxonomy and the taxonomy portion of KG use different algorithms. The README’s run options distinguish `TAXO` and `KG`; the output Wiki says `kg.json` is the hierarchy component of KG, while `kg.rdf` includes broader, part-of, whole-of, and synonymy relationships. [README](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron) · [Saffron outputs](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/3.-Saffron-outputs)
- The Wiki documents separate taxonomy scoring choices (`simple`, `transitive`, `bhattacharryaPoisson`) and search strategy. It specifically notes that maximizing transitive likelihood can produce overly flat taxonomies, motivating a non-transitive score; Bhattacharyya–Poisson additionally controls expected child-count structure. [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md) · [Taxonomy Construction](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/5.3.-Taxonomy-Construction)

### Inferences
- Use taxonomy links for exploratory “broader/narrower” navigation and optional hierarchical facets, not as automatically authoritative exclusion criteria. Keep `linkScore`, parent/child direction, run/config provenance, and an approval state; allow flat term filtering when links are low-confidence or reviewed as wrong.
- For SLR screening filters, store standalone taxonomy as its own relation type. Do not infer taxonomy edges from `term-sim.json`, whose documented meaning is similarity, or conflate `kg.json`/`kg.rdf` relation types with the standalone taxonomy.
- A review workflow should curate high-value domain concepts and taxonomy links before exposing them as canonical facets; support synonyms/aliases separately from parent-child relations.

### Gaps
- Docs call `linkScore` a likelihood/probability, but do not document calibration tests, universal acceptance thresholds, or expected precision by domain; therefore avoid treating it as calibrated confidence without local validation.
- Docs permit taxonomy relations beyond strict hypernymy (including part-of or domain-specific relations) but the standalone JSON node schema does not encode an explicit relation label per edge. The precise edge semantics can therefore be ambiguous for consumers; do not label every edge “is-a” without validation.
- The public docs do not provide quality benchmarks on research-paper corpora or guarantees that automatically generated roots are useful user-facing categories.

## Validation of the supplied SAFFRON 4.0 note and integration implications

### Takeaway
The supplied `SAFFRON_4_0_Features_and_Modules.md` is broadly consistent with the official module inventory, English limitation, term candidate/scoring flow, key artifacts, and taxonomy schema. Correct several output-name and confidence/edge-semantics caveats before using it as an implementation specification; best-fit deployment for SLR_Paper_Fetcher is a bounded Term Extraction → optional Taxonomy job over quality-gated metadata/text, with versioned results consumed by SLR API as enrichment.

### Cited Findings
- Official README lists the ten modules, including Term Extraction, Taxonomy Extraction, and RDF Extraction; the supplied note’s inventory is substantially aligned. [README](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron)
- Official Wiki confirms English as the module language and says the Web UI covers analysis only through taxonomy. It also lists Review mode as discontinued in the configuration Wiki index, so do not plan a production workflow around old Review mode validation fields. [Wiki Home](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki) · [Configure and Run](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/2.-Configure-and-Run-Saffron)
- The output naming differs among official docs: README lists `author-terms.json`, output Wiki lists `authors-terms.json`, and FORMATS uses “Author-Terms” without firmly resolving filename; README labels RDF outputs `rdf.json` twice, while output Wiki calls KG outputs `kg.json` and `kg.rdf`. The supplied note correctly flags naming inconsistencies; build adapters around inspected runtime output rather than assume all prose names are accurate. [README](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron) · [FORMATS.md](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/blob/master/FORMATS.md) · [Saffron outputs](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/3.-Saffron-outputs)
- README says all steps can run via CLI, and Docker can run a single module or pipeline; command accepts corpus, output folder, and config. Wiki says CLI/Web UI use cases; web-only output is under `./web/data`. [README](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron) · [Saffron outputs](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron/wiki/3.-Saffron-outputs)
- The official repository describes a Java/Maven application and says some modules use deep-learning models; installation notes first run downloads needed models and README reports approximately 3 GB free disk for Saffron/models. This is a deployment consideration, not a per-job runtime estimate. [README](https://github.com/insight-centre/saffron)

### Inferences
- Recommended boundary: `SLR_Paper_Fetcher` owns discovery, download, PDF validity, metadata normalization, and canonical paper IDs. Add a queued enrichment adapter/service that submits only normalized title/abstract (optionally validated full text) plus stable paper IDs to SAFFRON CLI/container; ingest `terms.json`, `doc-terms.json`, and optionally `taxonomy.json` back into a versioned analysis record. SLR API should expose approved term facets and reviewed hierarchy while retaining raw machine suggestions separately.
- Use `terms.json` + `doc-terms.json` first. They directly support term filters, per-paper evidence counts, and traceability. Enable taxonomy after term-quality review; defer KG, DBpedia, author-consolidation, similarity, and author-expertise modules unless a defined product use case needs them.
- Prevent junk/untrusted records before Saffron: require a valid extracted text body, language check (English), minimum useful text length, deduplicate by DOI/content hash, remove boilerplate/references where feasible, retain source/provenance, and quarantine low extraction-quality files. These are integration quality controls; they are not built-in assurances documented by SAFFRON.
- Store raw run artifacts plus normalized app schema (stable paper ID, term ID/string, aliases, counts, score/method, algorithm/config version, run ID, evidence source, review state); make filtering use normalized/approved data, never filenames or unvalidated model output alone.
- Pilot on representative known-good papers and manually judged terms/edges; select parameters and acceptable link-score threshold from measured precision/coverage, not SAFFRON defaults. Monitor failure and low-confidence rates and make enrichment rerunnable/idempotent.

### Gaps
- The user-provided note was not executable validation; this research did not install or run SAFFRON 4.0, inspect a fresh run’s artifacts, benchmark it on SLR_Paper_Fetcher data, or verify its behavior with current JDK/Maven/container dependencies.
- Public docs are inconsistent on a few artifact filenames and variant property names; confirm actual version/runtime outputs with a small isolated fixture before implementing ingestion.
- Integration throughput, operational compatibility, licensing fit, and quality on the project’s corpus remain to be measured locally.
